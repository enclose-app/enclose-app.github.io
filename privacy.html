enclose — privacy policy

Privacy Policy

Effective 31 July 2026 · last updated 31 July 2026

Enclose has no accounts, no servers and no analytics. Your walks, your claimed territories and your saved home position are stored only on your phone. Nothing about where you have walked is sent to us, because there is nowhere to send it — the app has no backend.

This policy explains what the Enclose Android app does with data on your device, what small amount of information necessarily leaves it in order to draw a map, and what your choices are. It applies to the Enclose app distributed through Google Play.

1. Who is responsible

Enclose is an independent app published by its developer, contactable at encloseapp [at] gmail.com. For questions about this policy or about data in the app, that address is the fastest route to a person.

2. What the app stores on your device

All of the following is written to private app storage on your phone. Other apps cannot read it, and it is never uploaded:

  • Walk routes — the sequence of GPS positions recorded while a walk is running, along with distance, duration, elevation gain and the time the walk started and finished.
  • Claimed territories — the shape you walked, its area, the name and colour you gave it, any notes you add, and the city name looked up for it.
  • A walk in progress — saved continuously as you walk, so an interrupted walk can be resumed rather than lost if the system stops the app.
  • A local profile — a randomly generated display name (for example "Quiet Fox"), which you can change. It is not an account and is not registered anywhere.
  • Preferences — your chosen map style, list ordering, the map camera position, and the home position you optionally save.
  • Cached map tiles — pieces of the map downloaded so the app works without a signal.

3. Location

Enclose is a walking app, so location is the point of it. Specifics:

  • Location is accessed only while a walk is running. Starting a walk shows a permanent notification for as long as recording continues, so it is never recording without saying so. Stopping the walk stops it.
  • Recording continues with the screen off or the app in the background — that is what the notification and the foreground-service permission are for — but the app does not request the Android "allow all the time" background location permission and cannot track you when no walk is in progress.
  • Your positions are written to the local database described above. They are not transmitted to the developer or to any advertising or analytics service.

5. What does leave your device

The app is offline-first, but a map has to come from somewhere. These are the only cases where anything goes out, and none of them involves sending us your data:

  • Map tiles. Map imagery is requested from OpenFreeMap, which serves OpenStreetMap data. Like any web request, this reveals your IP address and which parts of the map you are looking at to that provider. It does not include your recorded route, your territories or any identifier from the app.
  • City names. To label a claim with the place it was walked, the app asks Android's built-in geocoder to turn one coordinate into a place name. On most devices that service is provided by Google as part of the operating system, and the request is handled by the OS rather than by Enclose. If it fails or you are offline, the claim simply has no city name and nothing is lost.
  • Files you choose to share. Exporting a territory as GPX or GeoJSON, and sending it somewhere, happens only when you tap share and pick the destination yourself.

6. What the app does not do

  • No user accounts and no sign-in.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or telemetry SDKs of any kind.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data. There is no data flow that would make it possible.
  • No tracking of you across other apps or websites.

7. Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you remove it. You are in control of all of it:

  • Delete an individual territory from the app; deletions offer an undo, and are permanent once that passes.
  • Clear everything at once from Android's Settings → Apps → Enclose → Storage → Clear storage.
  • Uninstalling the app removes the database and all preferences with it.

Because none of it reaches us, there is nothing for us to delete on your behalf and no copy to retrieve if you delete it — including if your phone is lost. If you have turned on Android's own device backup, your phone's backup may include app data; that is handled by Android and your Google account, not by Enclose.

8. Children

Enclose is not directed at children and is not designed for use by children under 13. It collects no information from anyone, children included, but the app involves walking outdoors and is intended for a general adult audience.

9. Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR give you rights to access, correct, export and erase personal data held about you. In this app those rights are satisfied directly and immediately: the data is on your device, you can read it in the app, export it as GPX or GeoJSON, and delete it at any time. We hold no copy to disclose, correct or erase. If you believe otherwise, or want to ask, write to the address below.

10. Security

App data is stored in Android's private per-app storage, which other apps cannot read. The security of the device itself — your screen lock, whether the device is rooted, and any backups you have enabled — is outside the app's control.

11. Changes to this policy

If the app ever gains a feature that changes any of this — an account, a sync server, anything that sends a route off the device — this policy will be updated before that feature ships, and the effective date at the top will change. Material changes will also be described in the app's release notes on Google Play.
